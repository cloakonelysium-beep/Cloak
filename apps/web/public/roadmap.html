Roadmap

Privacy for Elysium. Fair launches nobody can snipe, and trading nobody can trace.

Live on testnet

What Cloak does today

  • Sealed-bid token launches. Everyone bids privately, everyone pays one fair price, and liquidity is locked from day one.
  • Private balances. Shield HYPE, USDC and launch tokens, then swap, send and withdraw without linking your wallet.
  • Batch trading. Orders are grouped and settled at one price, so single trades can't be front-run or traced.
  • Payment links. Send money as a link, split it into shares, or request a payment.
  • Receive addresses. A normal address anyone can pay, from a wallet or an exchange, that lands in your private balance. Bridging in from 20 chains through Relay comes with mainnet.
  • Private bids. Bid in a launch straight from your private balance.
  • Sealed NFT drops. Mint without gas wars, auction NFTs you already own, and sell whitelist spots fairly.
  • Uniswap v4 launches with creator fees and anti-snipe protection.
  • For builders. A TypeScript SDK and an MCP server so AI agents can use Cloak.
  • .ely names. Withdraw to a name instead of an address, and see names across the app.
Next

Mainnet on Elysium

  • Built on Gloam's live vault. HYPE and USDC sit in Gloam's main vault, on Gloam's audited code and final ceremony keys. Cloak's launch and ecosystem vaults are Cloak's own.
  • Deposit caps that grow with Gloam. They start small. Bigger deposits, trades and bids go in parts automatically, so the caps never limit what you can do.
  • External security audit of Cloak's own contracts, then higher bid caps.
  • Every contract source-verified, with a public 3-day delay on loosening any rule.
After mainnet

Growing the private layer

  • $CLOAK buyback. Half of Cloak's revenue buys back $CLOAK, and half of that is burned, by a contract anyone can check.
  • xStocks. Tokenized stocks, held and traded privately.
  • Graduation. When Gloam lists a token from Cloak's vaults, its holders move their private balances across, privately.
  • Spicenet. A second way to bridge into your private balance, next to Relay.
  • GLOAK. An experiment: a token that can only ever be held privately.
  • Partner launchpads. A shared private vault for tokens from other Elysium launchpads, so their users get the same privacy.
  • Creator dashboard and launch pages that keep working after a launch ends.
  • Private holder proofs. Prove you hold a token without revealing your wallet.
  • Notifications for bids, fills and claims.
  • Cloak's own Telegram helper bot. A helper in t.me/cloakdesk that answers questions from Cloak's docs, removes scams and leaked seed phrases, welcomes people, saves bug reports, posts launch and GLOAK activity, and gives the team a daily report. Built as its own service.
  • A team workspace on Slack. Shared channels with partners like Gloam, and alerts in one place: the keeper's health and gas, site deploys, test runs, and partner changes such as new vault limits.
  • More chains, one site. When Cloak goes beyond Elysium, every network lives at one address with a network picker in the header, instead of a site per chain.
  • A stricter security policy on the claim page. The page that opens a payment link will only be allowed to talk to Cloak's own servers and the chain, so even an injected script couldn't send a claim code anywhere else. Tested with every wallet connection before it goes live.
  • A multisig for Cloak's owner wallet. Cloak's rules, the vault guards, the trade fee and GLOAK's admin move to a multisig once one runs on Elysium (Safe isn't on the chain yet). Each handover is two steps and never touches users' funds.
  • Run-your-own keeper and relayer, so anyone can operate the infrastructure.
  • Compliance tools. Sanctions screening, and proofs that private funds are clean without revealing whose they are.
  • More names everywhere, so payment links and agents show who sent and claimed.
